## (a) The evidence-backed case for this section, phrased so it can be DRAWN

**The frame: a proficiency bar is cheap talk; a calibration card is a costly signal.**

Signalling theory (Spence 1973, *Job Market Signaling*) says a signal only carries information if it is harder for a low-quality type to produce than a high-quality one. A 90%-filled bar costs nothing to draw and nobody can falsify it — it is definitionally uninformative. A row that names the programme, the repository, the number and the date is expensive to fake because it is checkable. That is the whole argument, and it is a *picture of two objects side by side*, not a paragraph.
- https://www.sfu.ca/~allen/Spence.pdf

**The reader arrives already discounting self-reports.** Employers do not treat a self-rating as neutral evidence — they treat it as inflated by default: 80% of US hiring managers say resumes don't match real-world skills at least sometimes (34% "often/always"); 85%+ of recruiters believe candidates exaggerate listed skills; 86% say AI has made embellishment too easy; a 2026 survey of 1,500 applicants found 61% admit exaggerating expertise in a skill. (Vendor-run surveys — directionally strong, not peer-reviewed.) So the visual is not being asked to *introduce* scepticism. It is being asked to *resolve* scepticism that is already in the room, in the first second.
- https://high5test.com/resume-statistics/ · https://finance.yahoo.com/news/86-us-hiring-managers-ai-134500153.html · https://gcheck.com/whitepapers/trust-in-hiring-report/

**Self-assessment is a low-information channel even when honest.** The famous Dunning–Kruger curve is substantially a statistical artifact — autocorrelation plus regression to the mean (Nuhfer et al.; see the autocorrelation demonstration) — so "the bars are wrong because people overrate themselves" is a *weak* argument and should not be drawn. The surviving, undisputed claim is narrower and better: self-assessment correlates poorly with independent measurement, so an axis built from self-assessment has no unit. **Draw an axis with no unit, not a person being arrogant.**
- https://economicsfromthetopdown.com/2022/04/08/the-dunning-kruger-effect-is-autocorrelation/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8883889/

**Admitting the limit is what buys the credibility — but only in this order.** Pratfall effect (Aronson, Willerman & Floyd 1966): the blunder raised the *superior* confederate's composite attractiveness from 20.8 to 30.2 (+9.4) and sank the *average* one from 17.8 to −2.5 (−20.3). Competence must be established *before* the flaw is shown. The blemishing effect (Ein-Gar, Shiv & Tormala, *JCR* 2012) sharpens it: a minor negative helps only when it *follows* the positives **and** when processing is low-effort — i.e. exactly the skimming state a portfolio visitor is in. Crowley & Hoyer (1994) give the mechanism: two-sided messages gain credibility through attribution to the source.
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03342263 · https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-pdf/38/5/846/5085676/38-5-846.pdf · https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/20/4/561/1798572

**The time budget is real, and text loses it.** The Ladders 2018 eye-tracking study (30 recruiters, 10 weeks, hundreds of resumes) put initial screening at ~7.4s, up from ~6s in 2012; what *helped* was clear section headings, bold titles, bulleted accomplishments; what *hurt* was cluttered layout, lack of white space, multiple columns, long sentences. Treat the 7.4s figure as contested — Ladders is a commercial job site with an incentive for a headline number, and real screening time varies by stage — but the direction is not contested. Meanwhile the brain can identify a *concept* in an image at 13 ms (Potter et al., MIT), and Mayer's multimedia principle holds that words+picture beat words alone. A 3-line prose lede is the single most skippable object in a 7-second budget; a diagram is the only element that can spend 13 ms and return the whole thesis.
- https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/ · https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf · https://news.mit.edu/2014/in-the-blink-of-an-eye-0116 · https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning

**Scanning is layer-cake, not reading.** NN/g's 2017 revision: the F-pattern is a *failure mode* of unformatted text and costs comprehension; the effective pattern is the layer-cake — eyes hit headings and skip body copy. Their rule: headings must carry the gist in their **first two words**. Consequence for this section: the visual needs its own heading-weight caption, and the caption's first two words must do the work. "Calibration card" currently fails this — it names an object the reader has never seen.
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/

**Precision itself is a credibility signal.** Precise figures are read as *measured*; round figures are read as *estimated*, and precision anchoring measurably moves counterparties (~24% closer counteroffers in the negotiation work). "P95 under 200 ms across 10,000+ devices" and "~3 h → ~15 min" are doing credibility work that "Performance: ████████░░" cannot. The visual must let the reader *see the digits*, not a length.
- https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-small-big/201409/precision-and-persuasion-in-negotiation

**The metaphor has a real, drawable anatomy — use it literally.** An ISO/IEC 17025 calibration certificate (cl. 7.8, 6.5) must state: instrument identity; the method; the result **with its expanded uncertainty**; traceability to SI/NIST; the environmental conditions of the measurement; the as-found/as-left state; and — the load-bearing part — the **scope**: the parameters, ranges and uncertainties for which traceability was demonstrated. Everything outside the scope is explicitly *not covered*. That is why the metaphor is right and why it can be drawn rather than explained: a certificate's authority comes from stating its own boundary. The boundary is a shape.
- https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/98235-how-to-read-and-interpret-iso-iec-17025-calibration-certificates · https://www.accreditedlabs.com/blog/instrument-calibration-accuracy-traceability-documentation-explained

**The two audiences want the same object read at two different depths.**
- *Employer / hiring manager:* screening for risk of a bad hire under time pressure. Needs, in 5 seconds: is this role-relevant, at what scale, how recent, and can I verify it by asking someone. Their verification instrument is the **programme** (ATO, ANZ — verifiable by calling the employer). Practitioner consensus in portfolio hiring is consistent: depth over breadth, 3 documented things over 10 shallow ones, "what problem, what did you do, what came out" over aesthetics.
  - https://uxdesign.cc/only-30-seconds-to-reject-your-portfolio-8cb14ac70674 · https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/
- *Business client:* buying a defensible decision, usually inside a committee (Forrester's oft-cited ~13 internal stakeholders + ~9 external influencers), and leaning on case-study-shaped proof — problem → solution → outcome in a context resembling theirs. Their verification instrument is the **repository** (openable right now, at zero social cost, no reference call). Practitioner/vendor-survey figures (41% cite case studies as most influential; 73% call them a key factor) are marketing-blog sourced — treat as consensus, not evidence.
  - https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/social-proof-and-the-confidence-gap-what-cx-leaders-can-learn-from-b2b-buyers/ · https://www.highlypersuasive.com/7-powerful-psychological-triggers-trust/

**The one-sentence drawing brief:** *a bar has one end and no unit; a certificate has two ends — a source you can reach and a boundary it refuses to cross — and the picture must show the second replacing the first.*

## (b) The three questions a visitor is actually asking in the first 3 seconds

1. **"What am I looking at, and why isn't it the skills list I expected?"** — genre recognition. The visitor has a template in mind (bars/stars/percentages). Until the visual displaces that template, everything below reads as a weirdly formatted skills list. This must be answered *first*, non-verbally, and it is the question the current 3-line lede is trying and failing to answer.
2. **"Who says so — is any of this checkable without taking his word for it?"** — provenance. This is the question the 85%-exaggeration prior forces. It is answered by the *left end* of the picture: a named programme or a named repository, in gold, because gold on this site means exactly "there is a source you can go and check."
3. **"Does it cover what I need — and where does it stop?"** — scope and honesty in one. Employers read it as fit and risk; clients read it as "will this break on my problem." The pratfall/blemishing ordering says the honest gap must be *visible* but must arrive *after* the measured evidence, never before it.

## (c) What the visual must do — requirements that follow from the research

**R1 — Kill the alternative on screen, exactly once.** The visual must contain the rejected object (a proficiency bar) and show it being replaced, not merely be a nice diagram that happens not to be a bar. The thesis is a *contrast*, and a contrast needs both terms present. Concretely: a bar's fill length transforms into / collapses into a bounded row that carries a source at one end and a boundary at the other. *Because:* the visitor's default template must be displaced before anything else parses (Q1), and displacement is a comparison, not an assertion.

**R2 — The whole argument must be readable as a single static frame.** No motion may be load-bearing. The `prefers-reduced-motion` frame is not a degraded fallback here; it is the canonical image, and the animation is only its unfolding. *Because:* 13 ms concept recognition works on a still; and a 7-second budget may not include the animation's runtime, on any scroll position or connection.

**R3 — It must survive the layer-cake: caption first, first two words carry it.** The figure needs a heading-weight caption in `--font-heading`, whose first two words state the claim without the metaphor ("Nothing here is self-rated" / "Every row names…" — copy is the writer's call, but *not* "Calibration card"). "Calibration card" may remain the section title; it cannot be the only entry point. *Because:* NN/g layer-cake — headings are read, body is skipped, and a metaphor nobody has seen before cannot be the sole handle.

**R4 — The boundary must have area, not a footnote.** "What was not tested" must be a visible *region* of the drawing — the open caliper's dashed arms over the 45° hatch, given real geometric space — sized so the eye registers it as part of the instrument's spec, not as an apology appended at the end. *Because:* ISO 17025 scope is the source of a certificate's authority; and the pratfall effect only pays out when the flaw is actually perceived.

**R5 — Enforce the order: measured evidence first, admitted gap second.** In the animated path, the sourced rows must resolve before the open/pending row appears; in the static frame, reading order (and DOM/tab order) must put the gold-terminated sourced elements ahead of the hatched open one. Never open on the gap. *Because:* Aronson — the pratfall lifts the *superior* performer (+9.4) and destroys the *average* one (−20.3); Ein-Gar/Shiv/Tormala — the blemish helps only when it follows the positives and processing is shallow, which is precisely this reader.

**R6 — Every drawn element must terminate in something a stranger can check, or say why it cannot.** Each mark resolves to a named programme, a named repository, or an issuing body — and the pending one resolves to a printed reason. No element may exist that is neither checkable nor explicitly labelled unmeasurable. Gold appears **only** on the checkable terminus and nowhere else in the composition. *Because:* Spence — a signal is only informative if it is costly to fake; and this is the site's one taught convention, so the visual is where the reader learns to read it.

**R7 — Show digits, never lengths.** Any quantity in the visual is typeset in `--font-mono` at its measured precision ("P95 < 200 ms", "10,000+", "~3 h → ~15 min", "8 squads"). No fill proportions, no rings, no gauges, no needles, no radar/spider polygon, no 0–100 axis of any kind — those all re-import the unfalsifiable axis in a new costume. *Because:* precise numbers are inferred as *measured*, round/graphical ones as *estimated*; and a dial at 90% is a bar in polar coordinates.

**R8 — Serve both audiences inside one picture, distinguishably.** The source side must visibly separate the three verification routes already in the data — programme (ask the employer), repository (open it now), credential (check the issuer). An employer's eye should land on a programme; a client's on a repository; neither should have to read prose to find their own route. *Because:* the two audiences verify by different instruments and arrive with different budgets — the employer with a 7.4-second screen, the client with a committee to convince.

**R9 — Introduce no new vocabulary.** Use the Caliper's three existing states and the existing status glyphs. A fourth mark, a new legend, or a second colour meaning would spend the reader's one unit of learning budget on the wrong thing. *Because:* Ladders — clutter, extra columns and competing elements are what measurably lose the scan; and the site has already asked the reader to learn exactly one mark.

**R10 — Make the metaphor's mechanism visible, not its ornament.** Do not draw a certificate *illustration* (seal, ribbon, scroll, stamp). Draw the two things a certificate does that a bar cannot: it points back to a traceable source, and it draws a line it will not cross. If a viewer who has never heard the word "calibration" still reads "this claim is tied to something real, and that one is honestly out of range," the metaphor has landed and the word becomes a label for something already understood rather than a puzzle to solve.

**Failure tests to run against any candidate design:** (i) screenshot it at 200 px wide, greyscale, no motion — does it still say *not-a-bar*? (ii) cover the section title and lede — does a stranger still get it? (iii) does any gold pixel sit on something that is not a checkable source? (iv) does the open/hatched region read before the sourced rows? If yes to (iv), the ordering is inverted and it will cost credibility rather than buy it.
