# Copy style guide

## 1. Voice and stance

Write as a senior practitioner addressing peers. The subject is implied in achievement lines ("Led", "Architected"); first person appears only in the bio and principle cards. State; never defend. Trust comes from specificity: a named program, a number, a unit, a baseline. Every claim carries its own scope in the same sentence, so no label has to vouch for it. Verb-led, concrete, one idea per sentence, understatement over superlatives. The page is visual: numerals, diagrams and labels carry the argument; sentences carry only what a graphic cannot.

**What the benchmark shows.** The strongest public bios (bank CIOs and CTOs, Thoughtworks delivery heads, recognised AI practitioners) share five moves. They name what the person owns, not what the person is like. They place scope inside the clause. They give one concrete proof (a named employer, a downstream use, a duration) instead of an adjective. They open with what readers already know the person for. They close with a single channel. Current recruiter and executive-resume guidance (ExecuNet, Robert Half, Heller Search, Arielle) rewards measurable outcomes with scope, one positioning line, visible AI work and a scannable structure. It discounts unanchored skill lists, buzzwords without evidence, generic objectives and dense text. Learn the moves; never reuse anyone's sentences.

## 2. Anatomy and limits

| Element | Formula | Limit | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | Role · specialism · domain | 120 characters | Scrum Master / Project Manager · AI Solutions Architect · Government, banking, telecommunications |
| Positioning line | Role + domain + differentiator | 22 words | Delivery leader who architects: agile programs and AI systems, from mainframe test automation to multi-agent platforms. |
| Three-sentence summary | Who and where; proof with scope; direction | 60 words | See rewrite 2 |
| Achievement line | Past-tense verb, object, scope, result; tooling in brackets | 18 to 35 words | See rewrites 5 to 7 |
| Project one-liner | What it is, then its mechanism; status as a tag | 16 words | Job-application platform; an entailment guard reverts any claim the résumé cannot support. |
| Capability label | Noun phrase; tools only if searched for | 4 words | Mainframe test automation |
| Working-principle card | Imperative title (5 words), one behaviour sentence, one scope chip | 20 words | Measure, then claim. In Aether, market figures cite their source; an unmeasured signal reads "not measured", never zero. |
| Call to action | One verb, one outcome, one channel | 12 words | Book a 20-minute call. |

Sentence ceiling: 25 words. Paragraph ceiling: three sentences. Delete any sentence a graphic already states.

## 3. Numbers and scope

Number, unit, baseline and place share one clause: "from ~3 hours to ~15 minutes per scenario", never "92% faster". Percentages name their denominator: "≈92% less evidence effort across 200+ SIT scenarios". Use ≈ and ~ for derived estimates only; exact counts stand bare. Pair figures that depend on each other: "P95 under 200 ms across 10,000+ concurrent devices"; "$5M+ portfolio across 5+ squads". In a graphic a numeral never stands alone: value, unit, then a scope line of six words or fewer.

## 4. Simulated and non-production results

Add a two- or three-word tag after the figure, at the same weight: "in simulation", "test environment", "offline benchmark". Name what the source says was simulated: the CV places the −38% in a simulated production environment, so the tag is "in simulation", never "simulated budget". Example: "−38% error-budget breaches (in simulation)". In charts, use a hollow marker against a filled one. The tag replaces the explanatory sentence. Add no "not", "only" or "however", and no paragraph about method.

## 5. Australian English

-ise and -isation (modernise, visualisation); colour, centre, judgement, licence (noun). Use "program" throughout, never "programme". Dates read 10 November 2026. Figures take numerals and %; "per cent" only in formal prose. Serial comma only to resolve ambiguity. Titles without stops (Dr, Mr). Define ATO once, then use it.

## 6. Expert vocabulary

Use a term only where the work was done.
- **Delivery:** cadence, PI planning, capacity, critical path, dependency, go/no-go gate, contingency ladder, re-baseline, change request, traceability, escalation, SME enablement, environment stability.
- **AI:** orchestration, tool use, retrieval, evaluation harness, guardrail, entailment check, trace, regression suite, error budget, failover, verifier, specification.
- **Platform:** telemetry, latency percentile, containerised delivery, rollback, smoke test, quality gate.

## 8. Banned words and constructions

- **Defensive labels:** Evidence, Known limits, Limits, Caveat, Provenance, "What it does not do", "Where a reader can check it".
- **Hedges:** arguably, somewhat, tries to, aims to, hopefully, "not a product", "only", "I believe", stacked approximations.
- **Self-reference:** "this site", "the page you are reading", "quoted from a CV", "taken verbatim", "no score is printed", "not live activity", "Schematic ·", any sentence explaining the honesty or method of the page.
- **Clichés:** passionate, results-driven, dynamic, seasoned, proven track record, synergy, leverage (verb), spearheaded, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, thought leader, ninja, game-changing, robust, seamless, holistic, journey, unlock, empower, transformative.
- **Constructions:** "not X but Y" openers, rhetorical questions, three-adjective strings, "honest" or "genuinely" as intensifiers, more than one em dash per sentence, self-deprecating anecdotes, exclamation marks.
